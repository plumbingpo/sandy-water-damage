Bathroom Water Damage in Sandy

Tile, tubs and fixtures are made to get wet on top, but what lies beneath them is not. Once caulk splits, grout thins out or the toilet seal gives up, each shower and flush sends a bit of water through. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Sandy homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Signs to Look For

  • Baseboard puffing up or paint coming off near the shower wall
  • Caulk along the tub or shower that is cracked, peeling, or black with mold
  • Floor that feels spongy by the vanity or next to the tub
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • A hollow sound, movement, or loose tiles in the floor by the bathtub

What You Cannot See

What Hides Behind It

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. The water is category 3, and it rots the subfloor around the flange until the toilet starts to rock.

Mold in the Tiled Wall

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

Poor Ventilation

A weak or missing exhaust fan leaves steam in the room after every shower. Mold feeds on the moisture that lands on ceilings and walls, and damp materials dry far more slowly.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Supply valves and drain fittings under the sink can drip for months behind stored bottles and towels. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first few days

Water Gets Past

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Backer board and the outer rim of the subfloor take it in, and there is still nothing to see.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold gets going in the wall cavity and on the floor's underside.

Months one to three

Signs Start to Appear

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. Where a room sits underneath, its ceiling develops a stain that gradually spreads.

Six months or more

Structure Weakens

Subfloor around the toilet or tub turns soft and joists may begin to rot. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

How We Fix It

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

One by one we check the supply lines, toilet, shower and tub, then follow the water under the tile and into the walls with meters and a thermal camera.

2

Remove What Is Ruined

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. Where the toilet leaked, we treat the area as contaminated and remove porous material that cannot be cleaned.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the subfloor, the wall framing and the ceiling space below. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

After replacing backer board and subfloor, we can arrange tile work, a new seal at the toilet, fresh caulking and ceiling repair, which closes the leak path for real.
